DecodeDaily

A news briefing you can talk to. Listen while you're driving or cooking, and when a story uses language you don't have context for, just ask. It answers and picks up exactly where it left off.

The problem

News forces a bad tradeoff

Text-based news demands your eyes. You can't read an article while driving or cooking, so you end up defaulting to podcasts or radio. But those are passive and one-directional. If a story mentions something you don't understand, you either let it go or stop and look it up yourself. Neither is great.

The gap I kept running into: there's no news format built for eyes-busy, ears-free moments that also lets you ask questions. A well-informed friend can do this, they'll brief you on what happened, and when you say "wait, who's the Fed?" they'll explain and carry on.

This product was made with two people in mind. One is for younger audience who are told to read the news and be aware of what is going on, but the so called "barrier to entry" may be high for some. Another is also for professionals who want to learn about new things happening around the world that they may not have sufficient expertise in. News sometimes tend to use exclusive language that makes it a little harder to get to know the topic. Sometimes news describes what happened, but you want to know why sometings happened the way it did.

DecodeDaily's bet is that the right format is a conversation, not a broadcast. You listen to a radio-style briefing, and at any point you can interrupt with a voice question. It answers then resumes the briefing.

How it works

Producer, consumer, and the interrupt problem

The core challenge was getting the audio to be continuous and responsive.

I built the streaming layer around a producer/consumer pattern: one async loop fetches news stories and converts them to speech chunk-by-chunk; a separate consumer loop drains those chunks to the client over a WebSocket. This decoupling means synthesis runs ahead of playback, so there's no awkward silence between sentences while the next one generates.

Streaming pipeline: DB to gapless audio in the browser

The live path runs top-to-bottom, once per WebSocket session. The listener can interrupt at any point (left rail): the producer task is cancelled, the queue flushed, the question answered inline, then the briefing resumes at the next story.

On interrupt

  1. User speaks
  2. Server cancels producer, drains queue
  3. Injects pre-cached "thinking" filler no dead air
  4. LLM streams answer → TTS streams in parallel audio before full answer exists
  5. Resume from last_segment_id what user actually heard, not what was prefetched

Inside an interrupt: what the answer is built from

When the listener asks something, the backend builds a small context string and hands the model just two things: the question, and the one story that was playing. 

Context from current news:
  Currently discussing: {story title}.
  Content: {long summary, or full article text}   (first 2000 characters only)

User question:
  {what the listener asked}

In the prompt

  • The listener's question
  • The single story playing right now: its headline plus long summary, or the full article text when there is no summary
  • Trimmed to the first 2000 characters

Not in the prompt

  • Any other story in the briefing, only the one being heard
  • Where in the story the listener was, the playback position is sent up but never read
  • Earlier questions and answers, each interrupt starts fresh
  • Listener interests, source links, or article text past the 2000 character cap

If the server does not know the exact segment the listener was on, it falls back to the long summary of the last segment it sent. The prompt also allows general knowledge when the story context is thin, held in check by a single rule: never invent facts.

One more thing that I found out was that: iOS mutes Web Audio by default when the hardware silent switch is on. The fix is to play a tiny silent MP3 via a standard HTML audio element on the first user gesture. This forces the OS audio session into Playback mode and unmutes everything that follows. This was a platform quirk I found out the hard way.

Reflections

The product is far from perfect. Technically, the biggest limitations were around the listening experience. The robotic sounding voice did not make for a particularly nice experience, and when a user asked a question, the audio would get cut immediately, which made the interaction feel slightly unnatural. One possible fix would have been to have a visual cue to acknowledge that the question was received, and then let the LLM figure out a natural point to pause and answer rather than abruptly cutting the host off. More importantly, this was fundamentally a mobile-first experience, and being able to play it like Spotify while the phone was locked was a key feature that a small group of people who tried the app mentioned.

The bigger lessons, however, were around the product itself. When listening to audio news, people can quite easily "zone out", and some people actually lost interest listening to it once they zoned out. As people grow up, they generally know what to read, what to gloss over, what they can find out another time, and don't necessarily need every question answered. This made me realise that the TAM was a lot smaller than I had first thought. People don't just need to think this is a good idea; they need to be sufficiently motivated, and probably sufficiently disturbed by the fact that they are not understanding the news at the level they want to, to actually change their behaviour and use a product like this. What I feel now is that a product needs one of two things: either it delivers a journey that a user has not experienced at that level before, or it solves a pain point that a user spends a lot of time, energy, or money on.

Nonetheless, this was an interesting product to build technically and in the journey of finding users I have learnt a few things. I am open to feedback suggestions or thoughts on this product.
