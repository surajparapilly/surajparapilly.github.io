The problem

GP rewards a skill most students have never been taught

Singapore A-Level General Paper is challenging. It tests whether you can take a contestable question, interrogate its terms, hold two sides at once, and build a reasoned, qualified position. Students like myself, from the science stream have spent years being rewarded for correct answers. There is no “correct answer” in GP, rather it is about how you argue your point while maintaining balance, nuance etc.

The typical response to struggling is to get more content, more model essays, more examples. The most common study technique for GP that has been there since I studied GP 7 years back was to memorise a bunch of examples. None of that addresses the actual gap. The gap is the reasoning. How to autopsy a question, how to qualify a thesis, how to evaluate rather than just describe. These aren't things you can read your way into. They have to be practised. Regular chatGPT and Claude will bias to giving you the answer, this follows a structure to get you to think.

Socra's bet was that a Socratic dialogue, where the tool asks rather than tells, could build those moves through repeated practice. A student pastes a GP question and gets walked through a structured session: autopsy the question, lock a thesis, build an argument skeleton, deepen paragraphs. An essay blueprint assembles in the background as a side effect of the thinking.
